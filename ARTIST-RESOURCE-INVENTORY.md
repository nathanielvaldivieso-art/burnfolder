# Artist resource inventory

**Governing goal:** Build a reliable full-time livelihood from Nathaniel's art. The art is the proof of concept for any eventual Burnfolder SaaS; SaaS is tabled until the personal practice proves the artistic and economic thesis through completed work, retained rights, repeatable releases, diversified income, and adequate reserves.

**Purpose:** Working inventory of resources already owned or operational. Use this when planning projects, preparing funding requests, and distinguishing real production needs from unnecessary equipment purchases.

**Status:** Initial inventory — exact makes, models, quantities, condition, replacement value, and recurring costs still need to be documented.

## Strategic advantage already held

Together, these resources provide an independent production chain across:

1. Music creation and recording
2. Photography and moving-image documentation
3. Clothing and physical-edition prototyping
4. DJ performance and public presentation
5. Direct archival presentation through burnfolder.com

This means future funding can be concentrated on **artist time, collaborators, finishing, materials, access, presentation, preservation, and distribution** rather than foundational equipment acquisition.

## Priority uses of funding

Given the current inventory, money is most likely to create meaningful artistic leverage when spent on:

- Protected artist compensation and working time
- Mixing, mastering, editing, color, and other specialist finishing
- Paid collaborators, performers, fabricators, and technicians
- Project-specific materials and garment sampling
- Locations, permits, travel, sets, lighting, and production sound
- Storage media, redundant backups, repairs, and insurance
- Venue hire, installation, sound reinforcement, and public presentation
- Rights, contracts, accounting, metadata, and archival organization
- Small physical editions after demand is demonstrated
- Development of the next body of work after the current release

## Planning rule

Do not seek funding to duplicate equipment already owned unless replacement, redundancy, safety, insurance, or a specific artistic requirement can be clearly demonstrated. Funding proposals should frame owned equipment as an existing in-kind contribution and evidence that the project is ready to execute.
