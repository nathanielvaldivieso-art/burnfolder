# Music Career Legal & Business Setup Roadmap

**Scope:** U.S.-based independent artist / producer / label operator  
**Starting point:** Nothing formally set up  
**Last updated:** 2026-09-07

> **Disclaimer:** This document is a high-level checklist and planning aid, not legal, tax, or financial advice. Laws vary by state and situation. Consult a qualified entertainment attorney and CPA before making binding decisions or filing legal documents.

---

## 1. Business Formation

### 1.1 Choose a Business Structure

| Structure | Liability | Tax Complexity | Best For |
|-----------|-----------|----------------|----------|
| Sole Proprietorship | Personal liability | Simplest | Hobby / testing phase, not recommended once income or assets grow |
| LLC (single-member) | Limited liability | Flexible (pass-through) | Most independent artists/producers; recommended starting point |
| LLC → S-Corp election | Limited liability | More complex | Higher, consistent net income (usually $60k–$80k+/yr) |
| C-Corp | Limited liability | Complex | Raising investment, building a multi-owner company |

### 1.2 Form an LLC

1. **Choose a unique LLC name** in your state (search Secretary of State database).
2. **Appoint a registered agent** (yourself, an attorney, or a service like Northwest Registered Agent / ZenBusiness).
3. **File Articles of Organization** with your Secretary of State.
4. **Pay the filing fee** (varies by state, roughly $50–$500).
5. **Create an Operating Agreement** even if single-member; define ownership, profit split, decision-making, and what happens if the project dissolves.
6. **Obtain any required local business licenses**.
7. **File beneficial ownership report (BOIR)** with FinCEN if required (most LLCs formed after 2024 must file; check current deadlines).

### 1.3 Get an EIN

- Apply free at [irs.gov](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online).
- Use the LLC EIN for banking, taxes, distributor payouts, PRO registration, etc.

### 1.4 Open a Business Bank Account

- Use the LLC name + EIN.
- Keep all music income and expenses separate from personal funds.

### 1.5 Set Up Basic Accounting

- Use QuickBooks, Wave, or similar.
- Track: royalties, advances, merch, sync fees, expenses (studio, gear, travel, samples, marketing).
- Set aside estimated taxes quarterly.

## 2. Name, Brand & Trademark

1. **Stage / artist name clearance**
   - Search USPTO TESS/TSDR database.
   - Search state trademark databases.
   - Search Google, social media, Spotify, Apple Music, YouTube, and domain registries.
2. **Secure digital real estate**
   - Domain(s) (burnfolder.com, etc.).
   - Social handles.
   - Email using custom domain.
3. **Trademark registration (optional but recommended once name is established)**
   - Class 9 (downloadable music), Class 41 (entertainment services), Class 25 (merch).
   - File with USPTO or use an attorney.

## 3. Copyright

### 3.1 Two Copyrights in Every Recording

| Type | What It Covers | Owner Typically |
|------|----------------|-----------------|
| Composition / Publishing (PA) | Lyrics, melody, composition | Songwriter(s) / publisher(s) |
| Sound Recording (SR) | The master recording | Artist / producer / label / financier |

### 3.2 Automatic Copyright

- Copyright exists automatically when an original work is fixed in a tangible medium (recorded, written, etc.).
- You own both rights unless you sign them away.

### 3.3 Registration with the U.S. Copyright Office

- **Why register:** You need registration before suing for infringement; statutory damages and attorney fees require timely registration.
- **Timing:** Within 3 months of publication or before infringement occurs for full benefits.
- **How:** File online at [copyright.gov](https://www.copyright.gov).
- **Fee:** Lower for single works; group registrations exist for unpublished songs or albums in some cases.
- **Separate registrations:** Register composition (PA) and sound recording (SR) separately, or as appropriate for the work.

### 3.4 Documentation

- Keep dated sessions files, lyrics sheets, DAW project files, and notes of who contributed what.
- Fill out split sheets at creation time.

## 4. Split Sheets & Co-Writing Agreements

For every song with more than one creator, document:

- Song title and working title(s)
- Date of creation / completion
- Writer names and contact info
- Ownership percentages (must add to 100%)
- Publishing designations (if applicable)
- Performing rights organization (PRO) and IPI/CAE numbers
- Whether any contributor is a work-for-hire
- Signatures of all parties

Template tools: Songtrust, BMI, ASCAP, and various free templates.

## 5. Performing Rights Organizations (PROs)

PROs collect public performance royalties when your music is played on radio, TV, streaming (interactive), live venues, businesses, etc.

### 5.1 U.S. PROs

| PRO | Notes |
|-----|-------|
| **BMI** | Free for songwriters; publisher costs to join if self-published |
| **ASCAP** | One-time writer fee; publisher fee if self-published |
| **SESAC** | Invitation-only |
| **GMR** | Represents some high-profile catalogs |

### 5.2 Registration Steps

1. Choose one writer PRO and affiliate as a songwriter.
2. Register each song with accurate metadata, co-writer splits, and publisher info.
3. If self-published, join the PRO as a publisher too (or use an admin publisher like Songtrust / Kobalt / Tunecore Publishing).
4. Update your songs’ ISWC information when available.

## 6. SoundExchange & Neighboring Rights

### 6.1 What SoundExchange Collects

Royalties for **non-interactive digital performances** of sound recordings in the U.S.:

- SiriusXM satellite radio
- Pandora (non-interactive / ad-supported)
- Internet radio (e.g., iHeartRadio)
- Cable TV music channels

### 6.2 SoundExchange Split

| Rightsholder | Share |
|--------------|-------|
| Featured artist(s) | 45% |
| Copyright owner (label / master owner) | 50% |
| Non-featured musicians (via AFM & SAG-AFTRA funds) | 5% |

### 6.3 Registration

1. Sign up free at [soundexchange.com](https://www.soundexchange.com).
2. Register as both **featured artist** and **sound recording copyright owner** if you own your masters.
3. Add ISRCs for each recording.

### 6.4 Neighboring Rights Abroad

- SoundExchange-like royalties exist outside the U.S. through neighboring-rights societies (PPL in the UK, GVL in Germany, etc.).
- Admin services like Songtrust, Kobalt, or EXMO can collect these globally.

## 7. ISRCs, ISWCs, UPCs, Barcodes

| Code | Purpose | How to Get |
|------|---------|------------|
| **ISRC** (International Standard Recording Code) | Unique ID for each sound recording | Distributor often provides; or apply directly via US ISRC Agency at usisrc.org |
| **ISWC** (International Standard Musical Work Code) | Unique ID for the composition | Assigned by your PRO after registration |
| **UPC/EAN** | Unique ID for a release (single, EP, album) | Distributor or barcode vendor |

**Rule of thumb:** Use consistent metadata across all platforms and rights organizations.

## 8. Distribution, Publishing Administration & Royalties

### 8.1 Digital Distribution

- DistroKid, TuneCore, CD Baby, AWAL, UnitedMasters, etc.
- Compare annual fee vs. per-release fee, commission, and added services (YouTube Content ID, sync pitching).

### 8.2 Publishing Administration

A pub admin does **not** own your copyrights; they register your songs globally, collect publishing royalties, and take a commission.

- Songtrust, Kobalt, TuneCore Publishing, Sentric, etc.
- Useful if your songs are being performed or covered outside your home PRO territory.

### 8.3 Mechanical Royalties

- In the U.S., mechanical royalties for physical and downloads are collected through the Mechanical Licensing Collective (MLC) for many uses.
- Interactive streaming mechanicals in the U.S. are typically handled through the MLC or your distributor/publisher.
- International mechanicals are handled by local societies; a pub admin can collect these.

### 8.4 Sync Licensing

- Negotiate directly with music supervisors, publishers, or sync libraries.
- Typical deal: 50% master use fee / 50% sync license fee, often split between master and publishing sides.
- Have clean splits and clearance paperwork ready.

## 9. Work-for-Hire, Session Musicians & Producers

### 9.1 Always Use Agreements

- **Work-for-hire / service agreement** for producers, engineers, session musicians, designers, videographers.
- Specify payment (flat fee, hourly, royalty/percentage), deliverables, revisions, and who owns the resulting work.

### 9.2 Producer Agreements

Common structures:

- Upfront fee + no royalty
- Upfront fee + small percentage of net/artist royalties (often 1–5%)
- “All-in” fee including buyout of points

Agree in writing **before** the session starts.

### 9.3 Session Musician Releases

- Confirm the musician is a work-for-hire or that they waive neighboring-rights claims where applicable.
- Keep signed releases on file.

## 10. Samples, Interpolations & Covers

### 10.1 Using Existing Recordings

- Any recognizable sample usually requires **both**:
  - Master use license (from the label/master owner)
  - Mechanical / composition license (from publisher(s))
- Failure to clear can lead to takedowns, lawsuits, and lost opportunities.

### 10.2 Interpolations

- Re-recording someone else’s melody/lyrics requires a composition license.

### 10.3 Covers

- For audio-only distribution, distributors usually obtain a mechanical license for you.
- For video (YouTube cover), you need a sync license unless using a platform’s existing licensing deal.

### 10.4 Royalty-Free & Sample Libraries

- Keep license files for every sample, loop, or one-shot you use commercially.
- Verify whether the license requires attribution or has restrictions.

## 12. Taxes

### 12.1 Entity-Level

- LLC default taxation: pass-through (income reported on personal return).
- S-Corp election: potential self-employment tax savings but requires payroll.
- Consult a CPA before electing S-Corp.

### 12.2 Sales Tax

- Merch sales may trigger sales-tax obligations depending on state and nexus.
- Use a tax service or CPA to determine collection/remittance.

### 12.3 Estimated Quarterly Taxes

- Pay estimated federal (and state) taxes quarterly if you expect to owe $1,000+.
- Set aside roughly 25–30% of net income as a starting estimate.

### 12.4 Deductions

- Studio time, gear, software, instruments, repairs, travel to gigs, home-office portion if eligible, legal/accounting fees, marketing, samples/subscriptions.

## 14. Estate Planning

- **Will / trust:** Decide who inherits your catalog, royalties, and gear.
- **Beneficiary designations:** Update PROs, SoundExchange, distributor accounts, and bank accounts.
- **Catalog management:** Name a trusted person to handle your catalog if you become incapacitated.
- **Password manager / digital vault:** Store logins, contracts, ISRC lists, and copyright certificates securely.

### Week 1–2: Identity & Structure
- [ ] Choose and clear artist/LLC name.

- [ ] Form LLC (if desired) and get EIN.

- [ ] Set up quarterly estimated tax reminders.

## 17. Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **PRO** | Performing rights organization; collects public-performance royalties for compositions |
| **Pub Admin** | Publishing administrator; registers/collects publishing royalties without owning copyrights |
| **ISRC** | International Standard Recording Code; unique ID for a recording |
| **ISWC** | International Standard Musical Work Code; unique ID for a composition |
| **UPC** | Universal Product Code; ID for a release/package |
| **MLC** | Mechanical Licensing Collective; U.S. blanket mechanical licensing body |
| **Sync** | Synchronization license for pairing music with visual media |
| **Master** | Sound recording copyright (the actual recorded audio) |
| **Publishing** | Composition copyright (melody, lyrics, underlying music) |
| **Neighboring Rights** | Sound-recording performance royalties outside the U.S. |
